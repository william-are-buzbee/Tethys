# TAXA — the descent of the sessile life, and the order it arrived in

Design (9 Sep 2026), answered the same day and built as v11.15 — see [Decided](#decided). Downstream of `PLANET.md`, beside `FLORA.md` (which stays the reference for the six tests, the
bands, the grammar and the placement rules). This answers what FLORA does not: **where each form came from, what it split
from, what the split bought, and what reached the mountain when.** Nothing built from it yet; the person's answers go in
[Decided](#decided) and the build order follows them.

The brief (the person, 9 Sep): believability from evolutionary logic, not looks — every form has to eat and survive; the
order of colonisation matters (microbes first, and only a few clades ever stood up; Earth's Carboniferous shows the ones that
did could be huge); Earth's eras are inspiration, never a copy; and *real* splits — adjacent clades, several of them — not "big
plant, small plant, all from microbes".

Where things stand: FLORA's roster already says what each form eats and why it holds its spot (the "why it wins" column). What it
lacks is descent — five founder "lines" that are labels on the detailing, one photosynthetic line that does everything from a
crust to a tree by changing colour with depth, no order, no splits. That is the gap. This document is the tree.

## The rule of the tree

- **A split is an innovation with a cost.** Every branch is named for what it bought (a float, a runner, a stiff wall, a
  symbiont) and pays for it somewhere (drag, height, drying, light). No split for variety's sake.
- **Every species sits on a leaf, and every leaf names its food**: light, suspended food, dissolved food, or chemistry.
- **Convergence is marked, not hidden.** Three lines each invented a blade and a turf, because a blade is what light demands
  and a turf is what grazing demands. The player tells them apart by the line's signature, as with the creature clades.
- **Depth zonation is taxonomic.** The deep split among the photosynthesisers is *pigment*, and it predates the island: the
  open ocean's plankton already came in three pigment-kinds, each keeping a different slice of the spectrum. Each kind went
  multicellular on the mountain on its own and wins the depth its pigment fits. This replaces "one weed that changes colour
  with depth" (`pigment(h)`) with three lines whose colour is a signature and whose band is a consequence (Earth's proof: its
  greens, browns and reds are three kingdoms, their macro-forms invented separately, zoned by depth for exactly this reason).
- **Earth as proof, never as identity.** Where a tethys form and an Earth form coincide the physics coincided; the reason is
  written next to it. The five pictures (a Cambrian floor, two Silurian shores, a Devonian wood, a Carboniferous swamp) are
  read for the *problems* their forms solved: a floor of filter feeders with weeds among them; leafless jointed axes as the
  first things to stand in air; trunks and whorls before leaves; a swamp of jointed trees over anoxic water.

## The order — what reached the mountain, and when

A young oceanic volcano rises from the deep floor, breaks the surface, builds a shield, and sinks (PLANET, Geology). Each
band of life arrived when the summit entered its depth, and the bands have stayed on the flanks ever since: **the depth a
line owns is the order it came in.**

| stage | the mountain | what arrived | why then |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | a seamount below −450, in the dark | **mats** (grey, black); when the fissure opened, the **plume line** — crowns in tubes that farm sulfur bacteria — from the next vent along the ridge | vent animals disperse vent to vent as larvae; they are the oldest sessile animals on the mountain, older than its oxygen |
| 1 | the summit crosses the chemocline | **sacs** first (a bag with two holes needs only oxygen and drift), then **polyps** (the drifters were already in the water; a jelly that settled and cloned) | on every seamount the filter feeders come first: they arrive as plankton, and food arrives as plankton. Animals before weeds — the Cambrian floor |
| 2 | the summit reaches −150 | the **reds** (red; the deepest light) | the first light on the rock is red-blue and dim: a thin dark crust is the only photosynthesis that pays |
| 3 | −60 | the **floaters** (olive-gold); with them the **crowns** (ringmouths that sat down in the current) | the flanks now sit in the upwelling: enough food for a rope with floats, enough flow for a net of arms |
| 4 | −20 | the **greens** (green); the polyps take a green cell into their skin — the **reef**; the **cones** (hingeshells sat down in the surf) | bright poor water: green pays, and a polyp that farms a green in its skin beats one that fishes |
| 5 | the surface: a tall cone for a while, a real island | the tide band appears, 6–9 m of it, empty twice a day: a green with a skin that survives drying takes it, then stands up in air — **tidewood**; above the tide, on rain alone, a **scrub** and a **tussock**; if the cone held a crater lake, a **bog** | the intertidal is the road to land here (no rivers, no freshwater route: the land line is marine and salt-tolerant by descent — Earth's went by fresh water; ours did not) |
| 6 | subsidence: the drowned terraces | the land shrinks to the rim and the flank cone; the wood dies back to the tide band, the scrub to the rim's crest | **every land species is a relict** of a bigger island, which is why there are few of them and no land radiation to invent. The tide forest is a last stand, not a beginning |
| 7 | the flank collapse: fresh basalt | the pioneer sequence replays on the fan: rust mats → rinds → floaters → polyps in rust | the rockfall is stage 2–4 running again on young rock; its rust is the tell |

Consequences the world already half-has: the rockfall's rust (v9.5) is stage 7; the reef's lime and the fan's rust are the same
polyps on different rock; the plume at the fissure and the seep at the rim are one branch. What it adds: the slope between −60
and −150 must carry the reds (the oldest weeds, and today the slope has no weed at all), the shelf must stop being green
(the greens' band ends where the floaters' begins), and the land must read as a remnant.

## Four ways to stand

Standing up is expensive, which is why most of every line stays low (turfs, crusts, runners, mats) and only a few branches
ever built height. Each way has its place and its cost; nothing stands without one of them.

| way | who | where it works | what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a mineral skeleton** | polyps, the greens' chain, the reds' lime rind, cones | clear bright water (lime) or flowing water on basalt (rust) | slow; brittle in the surf; dead below −450 where the mineral dissolves |
| **floats on a rope** | the floaters | below the wave base, up to the surface, in fed water | drag: storms strip it; a rope in the surf snaps |
| **a stiff wall in air** | the greens' tidewood, reed and scrub | the tide band and the land — the one place a trunk to the light wins | dries; the roots stand in water only half a day; the sea takes it back as the island sinks |
| **time** | the sacs (barrel, vase, glass) | calm cold water: the terraces, the deep mud | centuries to grow; one slide ends it |

## The photosynthesisers — three lines from three planktons

Each line has a **signature** the player reads across its species (the way the creature clades read by eye, tint and
geometry), a **band** its pigment fits, a **founder form**, and a tree of named splits. Bands overlap at their edges: where two
lines meet is where the world is most legible (a green pasture giving out into an olive forest; olive giving out into red paint).
Colour is the line's, not the depth's: the greens shade green → olive across their band and stop; the floaters olive → gold;
the reds are red-purple everywhere and pale pink where they lay lime. `pigment(h)` became `pigment(h, line)`, clamped (v11.15).

### The greens — jointed axes, bright water, 0 to ~−40; the land line
**Signature:** jointed axes. Segments, nodes, stiff rounded parts; bright yellow-green (sunscreen colours in bright water);
mineral where the grazers are. The Silurian look in the two shore pictures — leafless jointed uprights — is this line's
signature carried into air: the tidewood is a jointed trunk with whorls at the nodes, not a bark-and-canopy tree.
**Food:** light, in the brightest water. **Holds by:** a holdfast on rock or a runner on sand; never a root.
**Founder form:** **turf** — a lawn of filaments (exists). The grazers' answer is speed: it regrows faster than it is eaten.

| split | what it bought | what it cost | species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the runner** | one giant cell running along the sand with uprights at the nodes: sand, where a holdfast can't hold (Earth's proof: Caulerpa, a single cell metres long) | no height, ever | **strap** (blades at the nodes; the flats' and the lagoon's pasture), **grape** (bunches at the nodes) — both exist; **paddle** (built v11.67: the runner's thick-bladed form for stirred water — a few broad short blades with a rind-thick cuticle that sheds sand and takes the scour, where the strap's thin blades are shaded and torn and the floaters' ropes snap; costs light per gram, so it holds only where `turb` is high in the light: the surf over sand — a scrap of ours, the giant's whole windward shelf) |
| **the stiffened axis** | a jointed upright that stands in the surf and the tide band | two ways to stiffen, and each pays differently: | |
| — with mineral | lime in the joints: grazer-proof, and its dead segments are the reef's white sand (Halimeda) | slow; only where lime is cheap: clear bright water | **chain** (exists) |
| — with a stiff wall | a skin that survives drying and a wall that holds the axis up in air: the 9 m tide band, empty of every competitor twice a day, and then the land | it must stand in water half the day or on rain alone; it is dying back as the island sinks | **tidewood** (`tidetree`, exists: re-read as a jointed trunk with a whorl of short branches at each node and a brush at the top), **reed** (new: jointed leafless uprights 1–3 m in dense stands on the sheltered inner tide band — the swamp you swim through at high water), **scrub** (exists: the dwarf on rain, jointed and dark), **tussock** (exists: a tuft of small jointed shoots), and if the crater lake is agreed, **bogwood** (a dwarf tidewood in brown fresh water, knees out of the peat) |

*Symbiont:* the polyps' farmed cell is a single-celled green relative from the plankton, which is why the reef and the
greens share a palette (vivid where bright, sunscreen colours) and a band.

### The floaters — a rope with floats, olive to gold, the fed flanks, ~−15 to ~−80
**Signature:** a rope with floats. A thin flexible axis, a gas float at every node or at the tip, blades hanging from the float
or the rope; no midrib, nothing stiff, everything bends and leans downstream. Olive to gold, never green. The Sargassum and
Macrocystis body, re-derived: buoyancy is free and drag is not (FLORA, Drag).
**Food:** light in the green-yellow that is left at −20 to −60, and the upwelling's nutrients — this line lives where the
current feeds. **Holds by:** a holdfast; or nothing.
**Founder form:** **wisp** — loose feathery filaments (exists; narrowed to this band and given two sizes: the ground layer of the
forest and its middle).

| split | what it bought | what it cost | species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the blade** | a flat thallus: the most area per gram | drag, paid by bending | **ribbon** (one blade on a stub, a float at its tip — exists), **ladder** (an axis with two-ranked blades in one plane — exists) |
| **the float** | gas in a bladder: reach to the light without a trunk | a storm strips it; stops at the surface and lays its blades flat | **bladder** (a column of floats — exists), **stipe** (floats at the nodes, spiral blades, to the surface — exists) |
| — **the drifter** | let go: no floor needed, the light at the surface, the eddies keep it home | at the mercy of the wind | ~~raft~~ — built v11.15, struck 9 Sep 2026 ("looks terrible"): the branch is real (Sargassum) but the surface is the drifters' now (DRIFTERS.md) |

### The reds — a rind, red-purple, the light's floor, ~−40 to −150, and the reef's glue
**Signature:** a rind. Flat, tough, thin; paint on rock, sheets, balls; dark red-purple; grows on other things; slow; lime where it
is bright. The last photosynthesis before the dark (Earth's deepest weed: 268 m, a red crust).
**Food:** the blue-green that is left below −60. **Holds by:** cementing itself to rock or to anything standing.
**Founder form:** **crust** — the paint (new: in FLORA's roster, not built; the slope has no weed today).

| split | what it bought | what it cost | species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the lime rind** | mineral in the rind: grazer-proof and surf-proof, and in the surf it *cements the reef* — a real reef's crest is not polyps but calcified red crust, because the crest is dry twice a day and pounded the rest (Earth's proof: coralline algae) | slow; bright water only | **limerind** (new: pale pink paint on the rim's crest, the passes' walls, the skerries; the polyps' rubble binder), **sandball** (new: a lime rind that let go and rolls — rhodoliths; the flats and the lagoon floor) |
| **the sheet** | a blade one cell thick, translucent: more light at less cost than a rind | eaten; the last blades | **redblade** (new: single dark translucent blades, −60 to −150, sparse) |
| **the deep rind** (built v11.67) | the founder's paint pushed to where the light ends — all pigment, a plate a finger thick with a pale growing edge; the floor of the light is the water's, not a number: under the island's fed water the reds stop at −150, under the basin's sparse clear water (`nut` 0.12) they pay to −170 | a hand's width a century; nothing else photosynthetic to shade it, and nothing to eat but the relict pickers | **darkrind**: the sill's drowned summits, which reach exactly −150 (SEAFLOOR §3's relict among the weeds — stranded as its shield sank); no island flank has water clear enough |
| **the epiphyte** (later) | growing on the floaters' stipes and the sacs: a free stand in the light | dies with its host | a rind on a stipe: the epibiont hook, first customer |

### What the three lines change on the map
- The shelf loses its green: turf stops at ~−22 (clamped olive at its edge), the forest's ground layer is the short wisp.
- The slope gains its weeds: crust from −40 down the terraces to −150, redblade sparse on the top steps, limerind on the crest.
- The lagoon reads as a green pasture (strap, grape, turf) with a reed margin at high water; the shelf as an olive-gold rope forest;
  the slope as red paint thinning out. Three lines, three looks, in the order they arrived.
- **The same lineage varies with the place** (v11.67, SEAFLOOR §2): the greens' turf and chain and the floaters' wisp, stipe, ladder,
  ribbon and bladder grow short, thick, narrow and heavy at the holdfast where the waves reach (`expo`), long, broad and thin in shelter —
  the three variants of a species are its exposure ecotypes now (grow.js `ecoK`), so a surf coast and a lee read differently in the same
  species. Fresh basalt (`young`) dulls a weed with its iron and rusts the animal forms; the tall floaters keep off flows younger than a
  forest's growing time (the progression rule: the rift arms, the fan's fresh blocks and the giant's flows carry turf, rind and rust, no
  forest). Neither is a species; both are why the giant's coasts and ours differ in their look from the same roster.

## The sessile animals — four lines, each with its splits

These already have clade homes (FLORA, Founders; PLANET); what they lacked was a tree. Short, because their clades' documents
carry the rest. **Food:** suspended (sacs, polyps, crowns, cones), dissolved (the frond), chemistry (the plume line, farming bacteria).

| line | founder form | splits | species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sacs** (the oldest animal on the mountain: a bag with an in-hole and an out-hole) | **tube** | **the stalk** (deep mud: a stalk clears the silt) → **glass**; **mass** (time in calm water) → **vase**, **barrel**, **cup**; **spines** (the shallows' grazers) → **burr**, **star**; **the bent tube** (a mouth across the flow whichever way it grew; a crown moves in) → **loop**; **the absorber** (gave up the holes: dissolved food, the deep's cheapest animal) → **frond** | all exist |
| **polyps** (a drifter that settled and cloned) | a solitary cup | **colonial** (clone and build): then **the fishers** (no light; flow-fed) → **fan**, **pen**, **tower**, **hair**; **the farmers** (a green in the skin: the reef) → **table**, **horn**, **dome**, **bommie**; **the floaters** (a gas float; farmers that also fish with lines) → **colony**, **colony2** — the surface's man o' war. The rust forms (`rusthorn`, `rustdome`) are the same species on basalt: the mineral is the water's, not the line's (PLANET, shell colour) | all exist |
| **crowns** (a ringmouth crawler that stopped crawling) | an arm ring on a stalk | **the open stalk** → **lily** (a net that costs nothing to hold; it watches, and folds); **the tube** → **tulip** (a bulb with a slit), the loop's occupant; **the bacterial farm** in the tube — the chemosynthetic branch, stage 0 → **plume** (the vents), **seep** (the rim; the straddler); **the prop** → **stilt** (a cone shell on two struts) | all built (v11.15.1) |
| **cones** (a hingeshell that sat down in the surf) | a plated cone | **the stalk** (into the current) → **nod** | both built |
| mats | bacteria; not a lineage | rust on young basalt (**beard**, **iron**), sulfur at the vents (**mat**), grey below the chemocline, the plate | all built (mat and greymat v11.15.1) |

## The places this makes

**The reef, as a real reef.** The caldera rim is the reef and it zones itself by the physics, once the reds are in:
the **crest** (the rim's top, dry twice a day, pounded the rest) is limerind and cones, no polyps; the **fore reef** (the rim's outer
slope) is the farmers — tables, horns, domes — vivid in the clearest water; the **passes** are the bommies and the fans in the
jet; the **lagoon** (the caldera floor at −22) is sand with the greens' pasture and dome patch-reefs; the **back reef**
(the rim's inner slope, sheltered) is the reed margin at high water. Spur-and-groove on the exposed flank is a terrain feature and
a later question. Nothing here reads a label; the envelopes and the lines do it.

**The swamp you swim up into** (IDEAS #5). Two candidates, honestly costed:
1. **The reed-bed and the tide forest at high water** — the inner rim's sheltered tide band: reeds in stands, tidewood trunks on
   their props, the floor a nursery (flickers and darters hide where the trunks are; the hunters cruise the edge). Cheap: two
   species and an envelope. It is the flooded-forest experience without a new system.
2. **A crater lake on the island's hill** (world.js: the flank cone is a 40 m hill; a cinder cone with a crater is normal). Rain
   with no outlet: a brown, tannin-stained, brackish pool over peat; the floor anoxic — a chemocline three metres down, in
   miniature; bogwood knees, mats, a freshwater turf; nothing lives in it that did not walk in. Derived, strange, and PLANET-true.
   **Not cheap:** the game has one water level; a lake at +20 is a second surface plane, its own fog and its own tint. Park or build.

**The surface,** two lines, legible: the raft is a floater that let go; the colony is a polyp that floats. Both farm the light;
only the colony fishes.

## Open — asked 9 Sep 2026, not to be decided by Claude

1. **Three photosynthetic lines or two?** Three (joint / float / rind) is Earth's real deep split and makes zonation taxonomic;
   two would fold the rinds into the floaters' deep end and lose the reef's crest. Recommended: three.
2. **Names.** green / floater / red (by signature, like ringmouth / hingeshell), or greens / floaters / reds?
3. **The greens' signature in air.** Jointed trunks with whorls (the Silurian–Devonian look) means rebuilding the tidewood the
   person liked (v11.2: "the trees look great"). Keep the silhouette and add the joints, or leave the tree as it is and let the
   land be the one place the signature is loose?
4. **The swamp.** The reed-bed (cheap) yes or no; the crater lake (a system) build or park?
5. **The story** — vent animals oldest, filter feeders before weeds, the land as a relict of a bigger island. Anything to change?
6. **Build order.** Recommended: the reds and the reed first (the slope has no weed at all; the crest has no glue), then the
   pigment clamp and the band narrowing (the visible change: the shelf loses its green), then the tidewood re-read, then the
   animal lines' missing leaves (plume, seep, stilt, nod) as FLORA already planned. Or the shelf first, since it is what the player sees?

start fresh with our thinking on plants". 4. The swamp is parked until the island is bigger (the reed-bed is built; the crater lake is not).
